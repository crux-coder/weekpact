Home pact cards

Six ways to cut the front card down. Same chassis every time — the tint, the 1px outline mixed .28 to black, the solid 3px bottom edge, an 18px corner, Rubik in 400/500/600/700. Nothing here asks for data Home does not already load.

What the card does today: title, icon, 84pt “3 / 5”, a segment per day, and a full-width button — in 200px. The number and the bar are the same fact told twice, and the icon floats in a corner attached to nothing. Every idea below removes something rather than rearranging it.

A

One fact

Drop the giant number. The segments already are the count — one box per promised day, filled for days kept — so let them carry it and give the space back to the title.

Cost: none. Pure subtraction from what ships today.

B

The number

The opposite cut: keep the number, lose the bar. One figure, one caption, one button — the card has exactly three things on it and the number owns the middle.

Cost: which days is gone; only the count survives. Reads well on a 5-day pact, thin on a 7-day one.

C

Ring row

The icon stops floating: it sits inside its own progress ring, which is the count. At 96px the card is short enough that the deck dissolves — three pacts stack on Home at once and nobody has to swipe to find out a pact exists.

Cost: the ring reads as a fraction, not as days; four-plus pacts still need scroll or a deck. Biggest structural change here.

D

Rows

The most minimal thing on this page: 66px a pact, the tint as a stripe rather than a field, the count as pips under the title. Five pacts fit where one card sits today, and the whole week is answerable in one glance.

Cost: the colour block goes, and with it most of the card's character. Efficient; less WeekPact.

E

The card is the button

Remove the button, not the number: the whole card is the tap target, and the state is the fill. Pending is the tint; kept flips it to cream with a green mark — the same cue _CompletedCheckIn uses today, at card scale. The cleanest face of the six.

Cost: one card-wide gesture has to serve check-in, undo and opening the pact — needs a long-press or a small edit affordance, and photo check-ins complicate it.

F

Week strip

Same footprint as today, one better fact. Seven weekdays instead of N anonymous segments: kept days are filled, today is outlined, days outside the pact sit faded. It answers “did I skip Monday?”, which the count never can.

Cost: Home has week.days() but the card needs per-day check-ins, not a total. Also the busiest of the six — it undoes some of the minimalism.
