Crew history — six more directions

What ships today is a stack of identical grey rows under the live week: date, seven segments, a percentage, repeated twelve times. Every week reads the same, so nothing in the list tells you how the crew has been doing — whether it is on a run, when it slipped, who carries it. These six give history a shape of its own. Each phone is drawn on the existing chassis (charcoal canvas, raised tinted cards, Rubik, the kept-day green, the streak orange, podium gold for a fully kept week). Tapping a week anywhere still opens the existing past-week sheet, so these replace the list, not the detail.

A

Season heatmap

One compact grid replaces the rows: a line per week, a square per day, shaded by how much of the crew checked in. Twelve weeks fit in the space four rows take today, and patterns jump out — "we always die on weekends", "August was rough". Three stat chips on top say the headline before you read a single square. Fully kept weeks get the podium gold on their number.

Cost: none on the backend — it is the same crew_week_history data (shareOut + percent) drawn denser. Best streak and average are computed client-side. Risk: 30pt squares are a small tap target; tap the whole row, not the square.

B

Bar chart + inline recap

A bar per week, scrubbable with a thumb; the selected week's recap sits right underneath instead of in a sheet. The recap is a summary, not the full calendar: each pact as a progress bar, and who hit their target as chips. The dashed line is 100%. The current week is the hatched bar on the right, so the chart connects back to "now".

Cost: per-pact percentages and per-member hit/miss for a past week — fetchWeek(crewId, weekStart:) already returns this, just summarised. Risk: horizontal scrub on a page that has horizontal swipes elsewhere; keep the chart outside the carousel.

C

Streak timeline

History told as a story: weeks are grouped into runs (consecutive kept weeks, orange flame) and breaks (the week it slipped, grey). Each group says one sentence about what happened — who carried it, what pact broke it. Long boring stretches collapse into one card, so a year of history is a short scroll.

Cost: grouping is client-side from the weekly percents. The one-line notes ("Mia missed 3 of no-phone") need per-week member/pact misses — a small extension to crew_week_history. Gain: the streak card on the live page finally has a past it points to.

D

Weekly recap deck

Each finished week becomes a recap card in a horizontal deck — the same gesture as the pact carousel, so nothing new to learn. The card is designed to be read, not inspected: the big number, a medal if the week was kept, the MVP, and a mini calendar per member. Swipe left to go further back. Shareable as an image later, for free.

Cost: MVP and per-member day grid come from fetchWeek(…, weekStart:); loads one card ahead lazily. Risk: only one week visible at a time, so trends are hard to see — pair with A's chips or B's chart above the deck.

E

History per pact

A History tab next to Members and Pacts, organised by pact instead of by week. Each pact card shows the last eight weeks per member, so the question history really answers — who keeps what — is one glance. A full square is a target hit; a partial fill is how close they got; a dashed square is before they joined. The bottom line gives the pact's hit rate.

Cost: a new query: per pact × member × week counts (one RPC, eight weeks). Slots into the existing tabbed crew page. Gain: the only option that shows individual history, which is what people actually compare.

F

Month calendar

A plain month view, the shape everyone already knows from their phone's calendar. Days are shaded by how much of the crew checked in; a fully kept week is wrapped in a gold band, so a good month literally looks gold. Tap a day and a small panel says who was out that day. Chevrons step months.

Cost: per-day shares already exist in shareOut; the day panel needs who-checked-in-which-day (already in fetchWeek). Risk: weeks are the app's unit and a month splits them; the weekly % column on the right keeps them visible.
